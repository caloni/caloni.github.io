Aqui devo registrar os resultados das minhas torras de experimentação. Onde quero chegar? Fazer uma torra caseira minimamente bebível. Tenho grãos não selecionados do mercado central de Poços de Caldas (MG) e mais dois sacos de grãos verdes de cafés especiais que obtive direto do produtor. Em ambos eu tenho versões torradas, o que será um teste muito interessante, já que o café de Piracicaba acabou se tornando um dos meus favoritos (ainda tenho alguns grãos torrados).

Bom, acho que alguns dados são obrigatórios: tipo de grão, data da torra, método da torra, peso de entrada, tempo de torra, peso de saída.

Outro dado importante será a data da degustação. Posso nomear minhas torras pela data e talvez a hora se fizer mais de uma torra no dia. Com esse nome seguirão as degustações com: data da degustação, nome da torra, método da extração, sensações na degustação. Se for uma degustação múltipla os dados estarão próximos para comparação.

Segue o primeiro:

- bean: "Poços"
  name: 20260826
  method: "Pipoqueira"
  input: 40
  time: 256
  output: 30

O livro de auto-ajuda de Dan Millman -- Way of the Peaceful Warrior (no Brasil O Caminho do Guerreiro Pacífico), que virou o filme Peaceful Warrior (no Brasil Poder Além da Vida nos cinemas, Um Guerreiro em Paz em alguns streamings, dizem) -- é uma fonte inesgotável de frases de impacto. Em inglês:

Core Philosophies on Awareness and Presence

"There are no ordinary moments." Every action, no matter how mundane, is an opportunity for presence and growth.

"Where are you? Here. What time is it? Now. Who are you? This moment." Identity is found entirely in the present instant, not in past regrets or future anxieties.

"You can live a whole lifetime without ever being awake." True living requires conscious awareness of the current reality rather than operating on autopilot.

"Take out the trash from what’s inside your head." One must clear out doubts, past failures, and future worries to focus on what matters now.

The Warrior’s Mindset and Action

"A warrior acts; a fool reacts." True strength comes from deliberate, conscious action rather than impulsive emotional responses.

"A warrior does not give up what he loves. He finds the love in what he does." Passion is sustained by engaging deeply with one’s path, not by abandoning it when challenges arise.

"A warrior is not about perfection, or victory, or invulnerability. He’s about absolute vulnerability. That’s the only true courage." Strength is defined by the openness to experience life fully, including pain and failure.

"There is no starting or stopping – only doing." Life is a continuous flow of action; there is no distinct beginning or end, only the present act.

"The accident is your training. Life is choice. You can choose to be a victim or anything else you’d like to be." Adversity is a tool for growth, and perspective determines whether one suffers or learns.

Wisdom vs. Knowledge and Inner Truth

"Knowledge is not the same as wisdom. Knowledge is knowing. Wisdom is doing." Intellectual understanding is incomplete without practical application and lived experience.

"I want you to stop gathering information from the outside and start gathering it from the inside. People are afraid of what’s inside, and that’s the only place they’re ever gonna find what they need." True answers and fulfillment come from introspection, not external validation.

"People are not their thoughts. They think they are, and it brings them all kinds of sadness." One must detach from the constant chatter of the mind to find peace.

"The ones who are hardest to love are usually the ones who need it the most." Compassion should be directed toward those struggling the most, not just those who are easy to like.

Life’s Rules and Acceptance

"Life has three aspects: Paradox, Humor, and Change." Paradox: Life is a mystery; don’t waste time trying to figure it all out. Humor: Keep a sense of humor, especially about yourself. It is a strength beyond all measure. Change: Know that nothing stays the same.

"The journey is what brings us happiness, not the destination." Fulfillment is found in the process of living, not in achieving specific goals.

"There is no greater purpose than service to others." Selflessness and helping others provide the highest meaning.

"Death isn’t sad. The sad thing is: most people don’t live at all." Physical death is natural; the tragedy is living without true awareness or purpose.

Sócrates e a origem das frases

Dan Millman (ex-campeão mundial de ginástica) escreveu em 1980 o livro como uma semi-autobiografia -- ele realmente viveu a experiência de encontrar um mentor em um posto de gasolina após um pesadelo, e estruturou essa experiência como narrativa de autoajuda em um personagem que é um misto de mestres que passaram por sua vida. As frases já existiam no livro; o roteirista Kevin Bernhardt apenas as reescreveu para os diálogos do filme. No final das contas, podemos dizer que as frases são de Millman, um autor que era atleta de elite e professor, o que faz sentido, pois ele transformou sua própria jornada de transformação pessoal em narrativa.
